# Simulation API

`src/engine.js` is a dependency-free ES module exporting `W=22`, `H=24`, `COLORS` (three CSS hex colors), and `Game`. Instantiate with `{mode:'storm'|'endless', variant:'wild'|'cadence'|'afterimage', seed:integer, practice:boolean}`. Variant defaults to Wild, preserving v0.1 behavior; mode independently controls duration. Practice suppresses ambient growth, automatic rain (including phrases), and the storm finish; it retains collision and skin rules.

`update(dt)` advances seconds through a fixed 120 Hz accumulator, capping each call at 0.5 seconds to prevent long background pauses from killing a run. `turn(dx,dy)` buffers at most two cardinal turns, rejecting reversals relative to the last queued direction. `shed()` returns whether an emergency shed was performed. `drainEvents()` returns and empties the event queue. `spawnDrop(x,color,y=-1,warning=.7)` supports tutorial injection; `paint(index,color)` and `resolveMatches()` are exposed for scripted lessons and tests.

Public state: `snake` head-first cells `{x,y,color}`, `previousSnake`, `dir`, `drops` `{id,x,y,color,warning}`, `debris` cells, `time`, `score`, `combo`, `bestCombo`, `health`, `status`, `shedCooldown`, `invulnerable`, `stats`, `stepDuration`, `moveAccumulator`. Colors use -1 for neutral and 0–2 for pigments. `status` is playing/won/lost. `stats` tracks caught, missed, clears, shed, removed. Removed counts actual length paid by matches and shedding, excluding collision penalties.

Events have `type,x,y,color,count,points`. Types are catch, clear, grow, miss, hit, shed, win, lose. Clear adds `combo,blasted,positions`; shed adds `positions`; hit adds `text` (wall/body/debris). Positions are cells for renderer effects, not mutable simulation ownership.

Additional v0.2 state: `variant`, `skins` (initially empty), `forecast` (initially empty), `phrase` (initially null). `skins` entries are `{id,cells:[{x,y,color}],arming}`; cells retain head-to-tail order at shedding. Positive arming means ghost, zero means solid. `forecast` contains upcoming notes `{x,color,index,kind,at,eta}`: `at` is absolute simulation seconds, `eta` is remaining seconds, `index` is the original 0–2 position in the phrase. Notes leave forecast when spawned. `phrase` holds the last announced `{name,kind,color,columns,startedAt}` even after its final note drops. `stats` adds `skinCaught`, `skinClears`, `skinsCreated`; body caught/clears remain separate. Skin completion does not increase `removed` because the cast's length was paid at shedding.

New events: `phrase` includes the phrase fields, `text` display name, first column as x, y=0, count=3, points=0. `skin_create` includes `skinId,positions`; `skin_catch` includes `skinId`; `skin_clear` includes `skinId,positions,combo,blasted`; `skin_reject` includes explanatory text and count=0. Hit text may also be `skin`. `paintSkin(id,index,color)` supports scripted lessons/tests using the same footprint and match resolution as falling rain.

# Mechanical details

The snake begins with twelve neutral segments, giving an immediately usable shed and enough collection surface to start finding patterns. Palette order is coral circle, blue diamond, acid stripe.

Body pigments belong to segment order during movement. A drop paints the struck segment plus its next tailward neighbor. Every contiguous pigment run of at least three disappears from the pigment sequence; the same number of physical tail cells retract, down to a five-cell minimum. The surviving pigments remap onto the retained path, with neutral cells filling the minimum body. This avoids spatial gaps and permits chain reactions across removed runs. Clears blast debris within Manhattan radius three of any matching cell; each clear wave increments a five-second combo, capped at ×8. Catching scores 15; clears score `(matched*100 + debris*35)*combo`.

Growth adds a tail cell at the next move, every 2.8 seconds. Motion rises from 6.45 to 7.81 cells/second across the storm. Rain accelerates from roughly one drop per .92 seconds to one per .42 seconds, with a .7-second column warning and 4.8–6.4 cells/second descent. First four drops use predictable columns and paired colors. Slag stacks above the highest existing debris in each column; cleared slag need not fall. A full column discards further droplets at the ceiling rather than creating invalid coordinates.

Collisions cost one of three health, trim up to four cells, clean radius two around the head, and provide 1.5 seconds of shield. Wall contact reverses inward and discards queued turns. Shield permits body overlap and destroys debris entered by the head. Consequently duplicate occupied positions can temporarily occur during recovery, but the body remains a bounded connected path. Entering a vacating tail cell is legal when no growth is pending.

Shed needs at least nine segments and a ready six-second cooldown; it pays four segments, cleans radius two around the head, and grants at least .8 seconds shield. Storm survival at 180 seconds scores a 1000-point completion bonus plus 250 per remaining integrity.

# Cadence and Afterimage

Cadence replaces Wild's rain generator, leaving movement, growth, catch footprints, matches and recovery unchanged. Each phrase announces all three same-colored notes 1.1 seconds before the first spawns. Sweep visits three neighboring columns in either direction; Echo revisits one column three times; Fork visits A, B, A with A and B two columns apart. The first three motifs are Sweep, Echo, Fork; later motifs use seeded randomness with no more than two consecutive repetitions. Color, direction and anchor are also seeded. Anchors stay in columns 2–19 so all motif notes remain inside the 22-column arena.

Note spacing decreases from .8 to .65 seconds over 180 seconds. A phrase's start interval is `max(3*(.92-pressure*.5), 1.1+2*spacing+.15)`, therefore about 2.85 seconds initially and 2.55 at maximum pressure. Only one phrase is announced at a time, though already falling drops naturally coexist. Due notes spawn with warning=0 because forecast already supplied the warning. This intentionally produces a lower peak drop density than Wild (roughly 1.05–1.18 drops/second versus 1.09–2.38). Cadence and Wild therefore are distinct rulesets, not a controlled experiment isolating predictability. Growing congestion and movement speed still rise. Practice suppresses phrase generation entirely.

Afterimage adds persistent shed skins to Cadence. A voluntary shed preserves all four discarded links in place, including their colors. Normal matches and collision trimming never create a skin. Head blast, cooldown, price and shield remain the same as ordinary shedding. A cast starts with one second of ghost grace; if any active snake cell overlaps it, its countdown pauses with at least .1 seconds left. It becomes solid only after the snake has cleared it. Solid skin head contact damages normally; shield permits passage without deleting the skin. Ordinary body/skin clearing blasts affect debris only, never other skins. Skins have no lifetime expiration.

A ghost or solid skin catches droplets at its cells. During overlap the active snake has priority. A catch paints the struck link, then soaks into the next different-colored link beyond its contiguous same-color patch (tailward first, headward if that side is exhausted), scoring 15 and incrementing skinCaught. Repeated pigment therefore travels through an existing patch. This keeps vertical skins solvable even when one link shelters the others from rain. Any three consecutive links of one pigment dissolve the entire four-cell cast. The burst clears debris in Manhattan radius three of every cast cell and increments the same five-second combo as body matches. Score is `(4*100 + debris*35)*combo`. The active snake does not shorten. Matching is also checked at creation, so a cast with an existing run can immediately complete.

Because shield permits folded paths, candidate cast cells can duplicate coordinates. Such casts, or casts overlapping existing skin/debris, scatter as a whole: the ordinary emergency shed still happens, but skin_reject explains that no artifact was placed. Individual cells are never silently omitted. Grace protects any overlap with the remaining active body rather than making the new skin solid through it. Growth, mode completion and practice otherwise behave identically across variants.

# Verification

## Relaxed pace (0.3)

`new Game({relaxed:true})` sets speed=.6, maxHealth=5 and growthInterval=4.2. `update` scales incoming real time before fixed steps; all existing rule timers and `game.time` remain simulation seconds. A normal Storm still ends at simulation second 180, which is real second 300 in Relaxed. Growth is every seven real seconds. UI displays time and recharge after division by speed; pressure, forecast and interpolation continue in simulation units. Training scripts use the same scaled clock. Standard defaults retain their previous values. Records use a `:relaxed` suffix, preserving all older keys.

Reproduce the exploratory policy using `node tests/policy-probe.mjs` (20 seeds by default; optionally pass a seed count from 1 to 100). The same module exports `chooseTurn(game)` for browser QA: call once after each head movement, then pass the returned `[dx,dy]` to `game.turn(...direction)`. It may call `game.shed()` when every move scores poorly. Browser import does not run the probe or import Node modules.

A bounded exploratory run across seeds 1–20 used an unshielded policy that avoided blocked moves, preferred open reachable space, and steered toward falling columns around mid-board. It completed 13/20 storms; losses occurred between 90 and 174 seconds. Completed storms produced 17–23 clear runs and 141–164 remaining debris cells. This establishes that catches and clears occur at useful frequency and three-minute survival is mechanically possible. It does not establish human difficulty, strategic variety, or fun. The policy did not successfully use shed; minimum-length periods often made it unavailable late in the run. No balance change was justified from this experiment alone.

Run `node --test tests/engine.test.mjs`. The suite covers input buffering, painting and chain continuity, body pigment movement, collision recovery, legal vacating-tail entry, debris stacking and catches, shed payment/cooldown, practice suppression, growth, seeded determinism across frame subdivisions, storm/endless outcomes, and twelve three-minute randomized invariant runs. Invariant runs use shield deliberately to exercise long simulations, not as evidence of playable difficulty or fun.

For v0.2 also run `node --test tests/engine.test.mjs tests/variants.test.mjs`. The unchanged twelve legacy tests plus ten variant tests cover full-storm Wild compatibility, forecast timing and motif geometry across 180 seconds, frame determinism, duration/practice independence, cast ownership, repainting/combo/debris clearing, body-priority catches, grace/collision safety, whole-cast rejection, persistence and absence of artifacts from involuntary shortening. Wild was directly compared against checkpoint 1dd42ad over every event and each second of simulation state; the permanent test records that baseline SHA-256 so source archives need no Git history. These tests establish rule behavior, not player preference or fun.
