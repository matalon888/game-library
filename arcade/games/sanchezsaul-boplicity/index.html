How scoring works

HOW TO EARN

The engine scores the bebop language itself — every value below was tuned from transcribed solos of the masters: Charlie Parker, Bud Powell, Sonny Clark, Barry Harris, Hank Jones, and Red Garland.

Enclosure +250 — surround a chord tone from above and below, then land it. Bebop's signature move.

Arpeggio +200 — three chord tones in one direction. Makes the harmony audible in your line.

3rd or 7th on a downbeat +150 — guide tones define each chord; the masters land them on strong beats.

Triplet +100 — one purposeful triplet per bar, the classic ornament burst.

Upbeat entry +75 — start your phrase on the "and." The masters enter off the beat three times out of four.

Chord tone +50 — any chord tone on a downbeat is a landing spot (half value on beats 2 and 4).

Sus 4→3 +50 — the 4 over a dominant is tension; resolving it down is the payoff.

Color tone +25 — b9, #9, #11, b13 over dominants (and the 6 on major chords): the colors the masters reached for.

Chromatic passing +25 — connect chord tones by the chromatic path, not the scale.

Rests are always free — space is part of the language.Let tensions resolve by step; notes left hanging earn nothing. Combos multiply your arcade score; the musicianship grade compares your line to the masters' own measured rate — 100 means you played at their level.
